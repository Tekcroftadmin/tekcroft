The Ecommerce SEO Problem

The Ecommerce SEO Problem & How Tekcroft Fixes It

Running an online store creates SEO problems that a typical service website simply doesn’t have. SEO for ecommerce websites has to account for everything from large product catalogues and category structures to product variations and search intent. Tekcroft addresses each of these head-on; here’s how.

The problem What holds your store back
What we do How we turn it into growth

Keyword Cannibalisation

Multiple category/subcategory and product pages can end up targeting the same commercial keywords, making it harder for Google to understand which page should rank.

Keyword Strategy & Mapping

We resolve this by keyword-to-page mapping and structured ecommerce site architecture.

Thin or Duplicate Content

Writing unique, useful descriptions for hundreds of products is difficult. Repetitive manufacturer copy gives search engines no reason to favor one page over another.

Unique, Optimised Content

Our focus is optimizing product and category content around product value and satisfying search intent with useful information the shoppers need.

Products Buried in the Site

Some sites have products 2-3 levels out from the category or homepage. Bad internal linking buries these products, making it hard to index and rank. It becomes more difficult when you’re managing SEO for large eCommerce sites with thousands of pages.

Smart Site Architecture

Tekcroft improves site architecture and internal linking so important products remain accessible to both search engines and users.

Traffic Without Sales

A page can rank well and still bring little revenue if it targets the wrong search intent. Someone researching a product needs different content from someone ready to buy.

Intent-Driven SEO That Converts

We align keywords and landing pages with the different stages of the buyer journey, from product discovery through to purchase.

The bigger issue in ecommerce SEO services isn’t just about ranking more pages, but about making them convert.

All of the above points need to work together to guide customers from discovering a product to comparing it and finally buying it.

Ecommerce SEO optimizes online shopping channels. It involves optimizing technical structure, categories and products, content, etc. The goal is to attract relevant traffic that converts and results in ROI.

The SEO services for ecommerce can cost anything between $1500 to $15,000 in the US. It depends on the size of your catalog, the level of technical SEO required, website competition, content needed, and the agency’s experience. The type of contract (retainer, hourly, or one-time job) will also affect the final cost.

Regular SEO covers informational and brand awareness pages, whereas ecommerce SEO is for high-density product pages. More importantly, ecommerce SEO targets intent to purchase, as opposed to traffic amount.

Ecommerce SEO takes time to show results; most online stores notice quick improvements in the technical and indexing aspects in the first few months. Ranking improvements, along with a noticeable increase in traffic, take 3 to 6 months, while significant, compounding effects take a minimum of 6 to 12 months.

All major store builders (Shopify, WooCommerce, etc.), open-source software (Magento), and even custom builds can use our ecommerce SEO services to increase sales. Our WooCommerce SEO services address both the platform’s technical setup and the content and structure of your store.

SEO helps increase organic search and is a free marketing channel for ecommerce. Users engage more with organic search results and then eventually make purchases. User trust in paid ads is low, and conversions are even worse.

Avoid agencies that:

  • Guarantee page-one rankings in a set timeframe
  • Cannot show real case studies or results from past clients
  • Offer prices that are too low for the scope of work
  • Cannot explain their strategy for your catalog and platform in the first 90 days

Category pages should be optimized first since they provide a means to target high search volume key phrases that lead to revenue from organic search. Product pages target specific and long search phrases that are more purchase-intent focused. Most stores benefit from optimizing both in parallel, but category-page work often shows results faster.
